# HORIZON — R&D and Prototype Framework

This document defines how Horizon can begin producing real work before it has the resources to build a property.

The website should make R&D a strength.

---

# Working prototype concept

Temporary project title:

**Horizon H1 — Intelligent Home Study**

This is a working name only.

The project can start as:

1. research;
2. architecture;
3. simulation;
4. Blender visualization;
5. software prototype;
6. sensor testbed;
7. optional small physical demonstration.

It does not need to begin as a constructed house.

---

# Core research question

> How can a modern home designed for an African context integrate energy, environmental sensing, water, connectivity, automation and AI into one coherent, resilient and human-centred system?

---

# Prototype layers

## Layer 1 — Architecture

Blender / CAD concept.

Research:
- orientation;
- passive cooling;
- daylight;
- shading;
- material choices;
- natural ventilation;
- solar roof;
- water storage;
- landscape;
- equipment room;
- sensor placement.

Output:
- floor plan;
- 3D model;
- exterior render;
- interior render;
- systems overlay.

---

## Layer 2 — Energy

Model:
- solar generation;
- grid supply;
- battery;
- essential loads;
- flexible loads;
- EV charging later.

Potential prototype:
simulate 24 hours of energy behavior.

Dashboard:
- solar output;
- consumption;
- battery SOC;
- grid import/export;
- cost;
- resilience.

Future intelligence:
- load forecasting;
- battery scheduling;
- anomaly detection;
- occupancy-aware optimization.

---

## Layer 3 — Environment

Sensors:
- temperature;
- humidity;
- CO2;
- air quality;
- light;
- occupancy.

Questions:
- How comfortable is the environment?
- Can the building reduce cooling demand before using HVAC?
- Can automation respond without annoying residents?

---

## Layer 4 — Water

Potential inputs:
- tank level;
- flow;
- pump state;
- leak detection;
- rainfall;
- water quality later.

System:
- observe;
- alert;
- prioritize essential use;
- detect unusual consumption.

---

## Layer 5 — Edge intelligence

Possible device:
Raspberry Pi or another edge computer.

Responsibilities:
- local sensor ingestion;
- local automation;
- offline resilience;
- device control;
- cache;
- secure gateway.

Critical house functions should not depend entirely on a cloud connection.

---

## Layer 6 — Digital twin

Create a virtual representation of the home state.

Potential display:
- 3D building;
- room conditions;
- power flows;
- water state;
- device state;
- alerts.

The first digital twin can be simple.

Do not over-engineer.

---

## Layer 7 — Resident interface

The experience should answer:

- Is everything okay?
- How much energy am I using?
- Is solar covering the house?
- How much water remains?
- Is the home comfortable?
- Is there an unusual event?
- Can I control important systems?
- What does the home recommend?

Avoid turning the house into a dashboard obsession.

The interface should surface information when useful.

---

## Layer 8 — AI assistant

Eventually:

Resident:
`Why did power consumption increase today?`

System:
explains the likely causes using home telemetry.

Resident:
`Can the house run through tonight if grid power goes out?`

System:
estimates battery autonomy.

Resident:
`Make the upstairs cooler before 7pm using the least energy possible.`

System:
coordinates shading / ventilation / cooling if available.

The prototype can initially fake physical actuation with a simulation, as long as it is clearly labelled.

# Research log format

Each log entry should contain:

```text
Title
Date
Status
Question
What I tried
What I learned
What failed
Next step
Sources
Images / model / code
```

This creates evidence of genuine learning.

It is useful for:
- Horizon;
- portfolio;
- university applications;
- future collaborators;
- future technical papers.

---

# Important principle

Horizon should not wait for perfect knowledge.

But it should distinguish clearly between:

- imagination;
- research;
- simulation;
- prototype;
- deployment.

That distinction builds credibility.
